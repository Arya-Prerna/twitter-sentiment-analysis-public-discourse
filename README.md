## Research Context: Crisis Communication during COVID-19
This analysis was conducted on a dataset from the 2020-2021 period, a time of significant global crisis (COVID 19) and heightened digital activity. This era was characterized by:
*   **Narrative Fragmentation:** The emergence of competing "contested narratives" regarding public health and government policy.
*   **Heightened Polarization:** An increase in politically charged discourse, as seen in the "Negative Sentiment" clusters of this project.
*   **Digital Public Spheres as Support Systems:** The use of social media for community wellness and "positive" narrative building (#healthy, #positive).

## Research Findings: Visualizing Narrative Clusters

A key part of this project was identifying the linguistic markers of "contested" communication. By comparing positive and negative clusters, we can see how digital narratives diverge.

### 1. Negative Sentiment & Political Polarization
The analysis shows a high concentration of political keywords and controversial terms within negative-labeled data. Terms like "politics," "trump," and "liberal" appearing in the negative WordCloud reflect the "contested" nature of social media narratives.

![Negative WordCloud](negative_wordcloud.png)

### 2. Comparison of Narrative Hashtags
By extracting hashtags, we can contrast "Community/Wellness" narratives with "Political/Contested" narratives.
*   **Contested/Negative Hashtags:** During the pandemic, health narratives often became entangled with political ideologies. The high frequency of political keywords in the negative sentiment cluster demonstrates how crisis communication can trigger secondary political polarization. (e.g., #trump, #politics, #liberal).
*   **Positive/Regular Hashtags:** Focus on social connection and wellness (e.g., #love, #positive, #healthy).
